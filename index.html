IMethod · the four parts of KITE Quattuor partes

A decision model as the behavioral kernel. The flagship only where the kernel is blind.

KITE is four letters and a kite drawn with compass and straightedge: the circles about K and T meet at E and I. Each letter is one part of the method, and each part has one measured fact.

  1. KKernel

    Ask each unique state once

    A decision model that outputs calibrated probabilities and never generates text (TypeSafe Jev) is the behavioral kernel of the simulation: each unique state is asked once.

    Epstein: 73,600 predictions, $1.60
  2. IIntervention anchors

    Spend the flagship where the kernel is blind

    A flagship LLM estimates intervention effects only on sparse paired anchors; the kernel's treated distribution is tilted to the kernel's control mean plus the flagship's paired effect.

    1,080 anchors = 1.7% of states
  3. TTabulated execution

    Run the whole population from the table

    Predict once, execute a million times: the kernel's cost is in prediction, not in execution.

    10⁶ agents × 20 steps = 0.90 s
  4. EError propagation

    Carry the systematic gap into every conclusion

    The systematic gap between model and people enters every conclusion as shared error. Every number is read on held-out human experiments.

    Coverage of nominal 90% intervals: 36% → 96%

IIHeld outSparse flagship calibration · Epstein et al. 2021 Calibratio

1,080 paired anchors, 1.7% of states, cut the kernel's intervention-effect error by 41%

9,070 participants, five waves, eight accuracy-prompt interventions, each wave with its own control. The criteria were committed before any result was read. The kernel predicted every persona × card × condition state once (73,600 predictions, $1.60); the flagship made paired predictions only for twelve states in each of six cells per wave, under the control and under every treatment. The kernel's treated distribution is tilted to the kernel's control mean plus the flagship's paired effect: a replacement, not an addition.

Nota bene · replace, not add

The rule replaces the kernel's treatment effect, so it also overwrites what the kernel got right: in wave 5 the kernel picked importance_norms, while the flagship and the hybrid both picked importance. This is the flagship's own discrepancy (β = 0.58) entering the conclusion; v0.2 carries it into error propagation.

Caveat · not shown

The pre-registered primary endpoint, within-wave policy value of hybrid minus kernel, was +0.003 [−0.012, +0.017]: across the five waves the best and worst policies differ by only 0.039, and even a 4,500-person human pilot sits near the 95th percentile of random policies. The power note anticipated this; effect error is the declared secondary endpoint.

Caveat · headlines

Headline-level heterogeneity (which cards respond more to the prompts) is not shown for the hybrid (r 0.16 [−0.00, 0.24]); the flagship audit (0.39) and a baseline-shareability-only predictor (0.38) are significant. The information is in the kernel's content model; the construction should become control distribution × shared effect.

IIIHeld outPaired anchors · SocSci210 · 37 unseen experiments Ancorae

Paired flagship calls for one to three personas per task, 0.5–1.5% of population states, lift decision gain from 0.27 to 0.39

Jev's native benchmark: 210 TESS survey experiments. The kernel predicts every respondent in the test set (136,901 predictions, $4.53); the flagship predicts only the first three respondents per task in hash order, under every condition (2,082 predictions). k, the slopes and the endpoints were set on the 91 seen studies and frozen, and the test set ran once. Captured gain is the share of the people's effect a decision-maker gets by picking the highest or lowest condition from the simulation (the G-A decision-value measure).

Nota bene · k = 1

One persona under each condition once, 694 calls, 0.5% of population states, already yields a gain of +0.114. Personas carry almost no individual information (r = 0.013), so one is enough: the tabulated tier and the sparse anchors rest on the same fact.

Caveat · unchanged

The within-task magnitude correlation r does not change (0.40 → 0.42), and raw effect MAE gets worse: the hybrid inherits the flagship's over-reaction (magnitude ratio 1.75; kernel 0.68). Direction and decision value come from the flagship; magnitude needs the discrepancy model re-estimated on deployment data.

Caveat · paired?

Unpaired anchors at the same budget (the flagship's first 3 people per cell) cannot be told apart from paired ones, and the direction flips between the two datasets. What can be said: 1–3 flagship calls per task buy flagship-level or better decision quality.

IVHeld outDiscrepancy propagation · fitted on 91 studies, tested on 37 Error communis

The gap between model and people is far larger than Monte Carlo noise; carried into the intervals, it lifts coverage from 36% to 96%

People's effect = β × model effect + a shared study-level term + a condition-level term. The three parameters are fitted by maximum likelihood on the seen studies (with the people's sampling covariance); on 37 unseen studies we read how many human effects the nominal intervals cover. Intervals with only the people's sampling error are false precision: a nominal 90% covers just 36%.

Act IIIWhy anchors · what the kernel sees and what it misses Actus tertius

Why anchors

It ranks headlines and situations well. It often misses which way an intervention pushes.

These diagnostics came before KITE. They show where the kernel is reliable and where its blind spot lies, and the sparse anchors are built for that blind spot.

VIIL1A methodological finding Tabula

The standard leaderboard's champion is a predictor that is useless for simulation

SocSci210's standard metric only asks whether each experimental cell's answer distribution looks like people's. Pooling people's answers across a study's experimental conditions as the prediction, a predictor that does not even know the condition changed, beats every published model on this metric. Switch to the two-dimensional view, which adds the axis "does the prediction move when the situation changes", and the champion drops to the floor.

XIL3Individuals Persona

Building a person: memory gives which questions move together, not a lifetime's temperament

Demographic personas carry almost no individual information for any method (cross-validated r = 0.013), so a simulated population that answers independently has no link from one question to the next. Letting each simulated respondent remember its last 3 answers, then applying a rank-preserving marginal correction per cell, reproduced the structure of the between-question correlations in all 6 new studies. But the structure is local: between distant questions, agreement is only about 40% of people's.

XIIL4Wind-tunnel simulation · a sketch Adumbratio

Three wind tunnels, one false headline: which one recommends the accuracy prompt?

Plug the effects measured on the calibration set into a spread network. Control and intervention use the same stream of random numbers, so any difference you see comes from the intervention alone. The first tunnel uses the effect measured in the human experiment, the second Jev's prediction, the third the flagship-tier GPT-6 Astra's; all three come from the same part of the 15-country held-out set. Near the tipping point the network amplifies a small individual effect, and the point the model cannot see is exactly the point that decides.

Method · Models and cost

Kernel = jev-1.13.0, phrasing p3 (one Noul per option), $0.023 per 1k new predictions, p50 213 ms. Flagship = GPT-6 Astra (high reasoning effort), run through a local Codex; at list prices it costs × the kernel per 1k predictions. GPT-5.6 Luna is the cheapest tier, not a flagship. Kernel spend on the three held-out experiments: Epstein $1.60, SocSci210 full population $4.53, the scale demo $0.32.

Protocol · Criteria and errata

Every held-out test's criteria file was committed before any result was read (Epstein, SocSci210 paired anchors, and the three Arechar held-out parts); errata are appended, never rewritten. Primary and secondary endpoints are reported as declared: Epstein's policy-value primary endpoint was not shown, and SocSci210's approximation-gain primary endpoint passed. Whether the intervention effects the flagship sees come from inference or from memory of the literature is not yet separated.
